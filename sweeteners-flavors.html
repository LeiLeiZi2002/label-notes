Chapter 04 / Sweeteners & flavor enhancers

Step 01

Start here

Sweet without sugar, savory without extra salt

Pick up a can of zero-sugar soda, a cup of light yogurt, and a tube of sour cream and onion crisps. The soda tastes sweet with zero grams of sugar. The yogurt stays creamy without cane sugar. The crisps hit harder on savory tang than plain salted potatoes ever do. Two groups of taste additives do most of this work: sweeteners deliver sweetness (with or without bulk and calories), while flavor enhancers, food acids, and flavorings sharpen savory depth, tartness, and aroma.

Four terms you’ll see in this chapter
High-intensity sweetener
A sweetener hundreds of times sweeter than table sugar, used in tiny amounts with little or no calories.
Sugar alcohol
A reduced-calorie carbohydrate (often ending in -ol, like xylitol or maltitol) that adds both sweetness and physical bulk.
Flavor enhancer
An ingredient such as MSG or yeast extract that amplifies existing savory taste rather than adding a separate flavor of its own.
Food acid
An acid such as citric, malic, or phosphoric acid used to add tartness, sharpen flavor, or balance sweetness.

Step 02

Ingredients & their uses

Part A · Sweeteners

01High fructose corn syrup (HFCS)

Hi-C, Minute Maid

A liquid sweetener made from corn starch that dissolves easily in cold drinks and gives a full, familiar mouthfeel.

On the label · Regular, non-diet drinks sometimes pair high fructose corn syrup with a small amount of a high-intensity sweetener like sucralose to cut total sugar.

Hi-C Orange Lavaburst · Minute Maid Lemonade Carton (59 fl oz)

02Aspartame

Coca-Cola Zero Sugar

A synthetic sweetener roughly 200 times sweeter than sugar, widely used in cold diet drinks. It breaks down under long, high baking heat, so it rarely appears in baked goods.

On the label · U.S. labels with aspartame must print a mandatory notice: "Phenylketonurics: Contains phenylalanine."

Coca-Cola Zero Sugar · FDA: Aspartame and other sweeteners

03Acesulfame potassium (Ace-K)

Coke Zero, drink mixes

A heat-stable, zero-calorie sweetener that can taste slightly bitter on its own, so makers often blend it with aspartame or sucralose to smooth the aftertaste.

On the label · Look for it right beside another high-intensity sweetener near the end of drink and dessert lists.

Coca-Cola Zero Sugar · Country Time Lemon Iced Tea Naturally Flavored Liquid Drink Mix · FDA: Aspartame and other sweeteners

04Sucralose

Country Time, pudding

A chlorinated derivative of table sugar that stays sweet under heat, making it common in liquid drink mixes, puddings, and sugar-free baked goods.

On the label · Often listed by itself or paired with acesulfame potassium; sometimes followed by "(sweetener)."

Country Time Lemon Iced Tea Naturally Flavored Liquid Drink Mix · FDA: Aspartame and other sweeteners

05Stevia leaf extract & monk fruit extract

Chobani Zero Sugar

Plant-derived high-intensity sweeteners with zero sugar calories. Blending stevia leaf extract (often Reb M) with monk fruit helps soften herbal aftertaste.

On the label · Often paired with texture helpers like guar gum from Chapter 03 on zero-sugar yogurt cups.

Chobani Zero Sugar Vanilla 4 Pack

06Allulose

Chobani Zero Sugar

A rare sugar that is about 70% as sweet as table sugar with minimal calories, giving yogurt and baked goods real sugar-like bulk and smooth body.

On the label · FDA guidance allows makers to leave allulose out of Total Sugars and Added Sugars. A "Zero Sugar" tub can still list allulose in its ingredients.

Chobani Zero Sugar Vanilla 4 Pack · FDA: Allulose labeling guidance

07Sugar alcohols (Xylitol, Maltitol, Sorbitol, Erythritol)

Ice Breakers, pudding

Reduced-calorie carbohydrates (not alcoholic) that replace the physical volume sugar normally gives to gum, candies, and puddings, often with a mild cooling feel.

On the label · Spot the -ol ending. Large servings of sugar alcohols like maltitol or sorbitol can cause digestive discomfort for some people.

ICE BREAKERS ICE CUBES Bubble Breeze Gum · Jell-O Zero Sugar Dark Chocolate Pudding Snack Cups · FDA: Aspartame and other sweeteners

Part B · Flavor enhancers, acids & flavorings

Why “natural flavor” and “artificial flavor” share a similar job

Both can add or restore aroma. U.S. rules distinguish them by where the flavor compounds come from.

21 CFR 101.22: Flavor labeling

08Monosodium glutamate (MSG)

Campbell's soup, Pringles

The sodium salt of glutamic acid—an amino acid naturally found in tomatoes and aged cheese. It boosts savory umami taste while carrying about a third as much sodium by weight as table salt.

On the label · When added directly on U.S. labels, it must be named in full as monosodium glutamate, not tucked inside "spices" or "natural flavor."

Campbell’s Condensed Homestyle Chicken Noodle Soup · FDA: Questions and answers on MSG

09Disodium inosinate & disodium guanylate

Pringles crisps

Nucleotide flavor enhancers that do little on their own but multiply savory intensity when paired with MSG or yeast extract.

On the label · They almost always appear side by side near the end of chip, soup, and seasoning labels.

Pringles Sour Cream & Onion Crisps · Official SmartLabel

10Yeast extract

Campbell's soup, Pringles

Concentrated yeast rich in naturally occurring glutamates, adding brothy savory depth to soups, sauces, and snacks.

On the label · Check the ingredient list for yeast extract. It can supply free glutamate even when monosodium glutamate does not appear by name. FDA says products with these ingredients cannot claim "No MSG" or "No MSG Added."

Campbell’s Condensed Homestyle Chicken Noodle Soup · Pringles Sour Cream & Onion Crisps · Official SmartLabel · FDA: Questions and answers on MSG

11Citric acid, malic acid & lactic acid

Pringles, Hi-C

Organic acids that shape tartness: citric acid tastes bright and citrusy, malic acid gives a lingering fruit tang, and lactic acid adds mild, creamy yogurt-like acidity.

On the label · Remember from Chapter 02 that citric acid can also bind metals in oils—check the food and surrounding ingredients to see its main role.

Pringles Sour Cream & Onion Crisps · Official SmartLabel · Hi-C Orange Lavaburst

12Phosphoric acid

Coca-Cola Zero Sugar

Gives colas a crisp, dry sharpness without adding citrus fruitiness, while lowering pH.

On the label · Appears high on cola ingredient lists, right after carbonated water and caramel color.

Coca-Cola Zero Sugar

13Natural flavors & artificial flavors

Country Time, Kool-Aid

Aroma blends that restore or standardize taste lost during processing and storage.

On the label · "Naturally flavored" on the front describes the flavoring source only; it does not rule out synthetic sweeteners or certified dyes in the rest of the formula.

Country Time Lemon Iced Tea Naturally Flavored Liquid Drink Mix · Kool-Aid Liquid Cherry Artificially Flavored Soft Drink Mix · 21 CFR 101.22: Flavor labeling

Manufacturer’s ingredient list

Umami enhancers

MSG and yeast extract supply glutamates, while disodium inosinate and disodium guanylate multiply that savory punch.

Food acids

Three acids layer the tang—lactic acid echoes cultured sour cream, while citric and malic acids keep the finish bright.

Flavorings

This label names each food acid and flavor enhancer. It groups the aroma blend under natural flavors.

Step 04

Review & practice

A common mix-up: “Naturally Flavored” means no synthetic sweeteners or dyes.

On Country Time Lemon Iced Tea liquid mix, the front says "Naturally Flavored," yet the ingredient list still includes sucralose, acesulfame potassium, and certified dyes. The phrase describes only the flavoring source.

Country Time Lemon Iced Tea Naturally Flavored Liquid Drink Mix

A common mix-up: “No MSG Added” means zero glutamates.

A soup can skip pure monosodium glutamate and still rely on yeast extract, tomato paste, or cheese for naturally occurring glutamates.

Check for those ingredients directly. FDA says products containing sources of naturally occurring free glutamate cannot claim “No MSG” or “No MSG Added.”

FDA: Questions and answers on MSG

Where to look when the front says “Zero Sugar”

“Zero Sugar” describes sugars, not whether the product uses sweeteners. Check the ingredient list for names such as aspartame, sucralose, or stevia extract.

Coca-Cola Zero Sugar · Chobani Zero Sugar Vanilla 4 Pack
