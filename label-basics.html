Reading an ingredient list

Before learning individual additives, it helps to know how U.S. ingredient lists are organized. Four quick rules cover almost every package in the store.

01

Listed by weight

Ingredients appear in descending order of weight, from heaviest to lightest. Main bases like flour or water come first; most additives sit near the end.

Whole wheat flour, water, …

02

The "2% or less" line

Phrases like "Contains 2% or less of:" mark a cutoff where small-amount ingredients begin. Additives cluster here, though salt and spices often appear here too.

Contains 2% or less of:

03

Two kinds of parentheses

Parentheses either break down a compound ingredient—such as enriched flour (flour, niacin, iron)—or state an additive's job, such as "(to extend freshness)."

(to extend freshness)

04

What a label can and cannot tell you

A label shows what is in the recipe, rough order by weight, major allergens ("Contains: wheat, milk, soy"), and some purpose phrases. It does not list exact percentages or tell you whether a whole food is healthy.

Contains wheat, milk, soy.

Worked example

Farmhouse Whole Grain White Bread

Whole wheat flour, water, enriched wheat flour (flour, niacin, reduced iron, thiamine mononitrate, riboflavin, folic acid), sugar, oat fiber, yeast, wheat gluten, soybean oil, contains 2% or less of: salt, whey, calcium propionate and sorbic acid to extend freshness, monoglycerides, sodium stearoyl lactylate, DATEM, soy lecithin, citric acid, malted barley flour, butter. Contains wheat, milk, soy.

Weight order

Whole wheat flour and water appear first because they are the two heaviest ingredients.

2% cutoff

Each ingredient after this cutoff makes up no more than 2% by weight. Those ingredients may be listed in a different order.

Parentheses

These parentheses list what goes into the enriched flour. The phrase “to extend freshness” later in the list gives the purpose of two preservatives.

Allergen line

The final Contains line lists the major allergens declared on this package.
