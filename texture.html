Chapter 03 / Emulsifiers, stabilizers & thickeners

Step 01

Start here

Shake oil and vinegar in a jar, then set it down. The oil rises again. A creamy ranch dressing stays mixed much longer.

Emulsifiers help oil and water stay mixed. Egg yolk in mayonnaise and polysorbate 60 in ranch dressing both help keep oil droplets dispersed.

Yogurt can collect a watery layer, and frozen desserts can turn grainy. Stabilizers and thickeners can hold water, keep particles suspended or limit ice crystal growth.

Four terms you’ll see in this chapter
Emulsion
Tiny droplets of one liquid spread through another, as in oil and vinegar after shaking.
Emulsifier
An ingredient that helps the droplets stay dispersed.
Thickener
An ingredient that gives a liquid more body.
Stabilizer
An ingredient that helps the texture stay even, for example by slowing settling or ice crystal growth.

PGPR helps melted chocolate start flowing, which makes it easier to fill a thin mold.

On the label · Look for PGPR near Lecithin (Soy). It stands for polyglycerol polyricinoleate.

Why “gum” is not chewing gum

Food gums can come from seeds, seaweed or fermentation. They interact with water, giving foods body or helping the texture stay even.

07Xanthan gum

Kraft ranch dressing

Xanthan gum thickens cold dressing and helps keep chopped herbs and garlic suspended.

Without it · A thinner dressing runs off lettuce faster, and small pieces can sink while the bottle sits.

On the label · Look for Xanthan gum near Garlic. The dressing also lists modified food starch, which adds body.

11Pectin

Yoplait strawberry yogurt and grape jelly

Pectin forms a soft gel that gives fruit yogurt and jelly body.

Without it · In recipes that use pectin to set, the jelly can run and the yogurt can be thinner.

On the label · Look for Fruit pectin on the Yoplait list. In fruit jellies, it often appears simply as Pectin.

Starch adds body and helps hold water. In fruit yogurt, it can work alongside pectin to reduce watery separation.

Without it · Clear liquid can pool on top of the yogurt, and a low-fat dressing can feel thin.

On the label · Yoplait specifies Modified corn starch. Kraft lists Modified food starch without naming a starch source.

Step 03

Read a food label

A single ingredient list can include several texture ingredients alongside preservatives. Use this ranch dressing to see where each group appears.

Four steps for reading a label

  1. Start with the full ingredient names.

    Find egg yolks, polysorbate 60, modified food starch and xanthan gum.

  2. Separate the jobs.

    Egg yolks and polysorbate 60 help disperse oil droplets. Starch adds body, and xanthan gum helps hold the chopped herbs and garlic in place.

  3. Read the purpose statement.

    Near the end, potassium sorbate and calcium disodium EDTA appear together with “to protect flavor.”

  4. Check which change each ingredient slows.

    Sorbate helps control microbes. EDTA binds trace metals that can speed oxidation.

Step 04

Review & practice

A common mix-up: “Oil on top means the peanut butter has gone bad.”

Oil naturally rises in peanut butter made with just peanuts and salt. Stir it back in. The oil layer on its own does not mean the peanut butter has gone bad.

Smucker’s Organic Creamy Peanut Butter

A common mix-up: “Gums and starch are just fillers.”

Several gums can work together in small amounts to control texture. Counting the names on a label does not tell you whether the food is safe or healthy.

In yogurt, pectin and starch help hold water. In a frozen dessert, a blend of gums can help keep ice crystals small.

Silk says its almondmilk is free from carrageenan and lists gellan gum, another stabilizer. A claim about one gum leaves room for others in the recipe.
